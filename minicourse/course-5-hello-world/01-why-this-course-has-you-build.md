# 01 — Why This Course Has You Build, Not Watch

Every course so far taught you the method's mechanics through one build someone else already finished — and each one already ended with an Exercise lesson sending you off to run the same steps on an idea of your own. So this isn't the first time you're building something. What's been missing is the chance to see the *whole* loop at once, on one continuous build, with enough guidance at every step that you're never stuck translating a generic instruction into your own case. The per-course Exercises are compressed by necessity — a checklist at the end of a course, covering ground you've already read about in detail. This course inverts that: ten lessons, each one small, each one telling you exactly what to run and what to expect before handing you back to your own idea.

That matters because of a question the per-course Exercises can't fully answer on their own: how much of what you saw in Courses 1-4 was the *method* doing the work, versus the worked example being interesting? If a worked example turns out well, was that because the plugin is good at its job, or because the example was chosen to work well? Starting in Lesson 03, every lesson here ends with an explicit instruction — run this command now, on your own idea, then come back here for the next step. By the time you reach Lesson 09, you'll be looking at your own spec, your own demolition cycle, your own macro table, built end to end in one sitting instead of spread across four separate Exercises — and you'll be able to point at specific answers you gave that explain specific things in what got built.

## What you're actually building toward

A **Custom Scorecard** — the app category Lesson 02 covers in full. Small on purpose: no external services, no paid APIs, no AI running at runtime, realistically a few hours of build time once your spec is frozen. The smallness is the point. If your own build still ends up with real content — a vulnerability that actually mattered when demolished, a version bump that reflects a real change, a macro table shaped by things specific to your domain — none of that can be explained away as "the app was complicated." It has to be coming from your answers, because there's nowhere else it could come from.

## How the lessons are structured from here

Lessons 03 through 08 each map to one real step in the loop you already know from Courses 1 and 2: onboarding, persona selection, demolition, integration, spec-to-code, deploy. Each one explains the mechanic in general terms, gives you a sense of how the same question can land differently depending on what you're actually building, and then hands you back to the plugin with a concrete instruction. Lesson 09 asks you to look back at your own trail of decisions and name, explicitly, where a different answer would have changed the outcome. Lesson 10 closes out both this course and the five-course sequence.

If you're doing this alongside someone else — a classmate, a colleague, anyone else working through this course on a different Custom Scorecard idea — that's a genuine bonus for Lesson 09: comparing two real builds side by side makes the divergence even easier to see. But it's not required. The proof this course is after doesn't need a second person. It needs you to actually run the commands.

- Before you've answered a single onboarding question, what's one thing about your own Custom Scorecard idea that you'd expect to shape the build in a way a generic template couldn't predict?
