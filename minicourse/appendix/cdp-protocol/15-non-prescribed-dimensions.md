# 15 — The Five Non-Prescribed Dimensions

🎬 Video: [`video-prompts.md#15-non-prescribed-dimensions`](video-prompts.md#15-non-prescribed-dimensions)

---

The manifesto declares procedures precisely. This lesson is about what it deliberately does *not* declare, and why leaving those things unlegislated is a design choice, not an oversight.

The CDP refuses to legislate five dimensions of its own operation. Each is sized by the author or organization to the artifact and context. Legislating any of them across every domain would distort at least one domain the CDP is meant to serve.

## (a) What "hardened" substantively means

The four exit criteria (Lesson 13) are procedural — they confirm the method ran end to end. They don't prescribe what "hardened" looks like *substantively*, because the robustness bar for a software spec, a policy document, a philosophical essay, and a long-form book differs in kind, not just degree. A universal rubric would either dilute to meaninglessness or distort at least one domain. So: the author declares, at exit, the context-specific criteria that make the spec hardened for its intended use — and logs them in the demolition history.

## (b) & (e) Round duration and total time-to-value

The time a round takes is a diagnostic *output*, not a prescribed input. A fast round signals a spec already robust on that angle; a long round signals the persona found real material. The same logic scales up to total project time-to-value. The protocol doesn't budget calendar time at either scale — it offers the convergence signal from Lesson 13 instead. Budgeting the CDP in calendar time misstates the primitive: it's scoped by the attacks planned and the convergence of vulnerability rates, not by the clock.

## (c) Cost

Cost is a property of the embodiment you chose (Lesson 06), not of the protocol. A self-hosted open-weight model approaches zero marginal cost; a commercial mid-tier model scales with that vendor's pricing; a flagship model costs more and is usually unnecessary. Quantifying cost at the protocol level would bind the CDP to one pricing regime — the exact lock-in platform-agnosticism is designed to avoid.

## (d) The round count

N's default of 3 (Lesson 13) is a starting convention, not an empirically distilled minimum. Organizations can mandate higher minima and persona-coverage requirements as governance layered on top; individual authors can set their own N sized to their stakes. This is the legitimate interface between organizational policy and the protocol — not a gap the protocol failed to close.

## The pattern across all five

Notice what these five have in common: each is a place where a single hard-coded rule would work *somewhere* and fail *everywhere else*. The manifesto's response isn't silence — it's an explicit refusal to legislate, paired with an observational signal (the convergence heuristic) where one exists. That's a different thing from an unfinished spec, and it's worth being able to tell the two apart when you're reading any specification, not just this one.
