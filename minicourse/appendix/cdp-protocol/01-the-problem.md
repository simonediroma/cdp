# 01 — The Problem

🎬 Video: [`video-prompts.md#01-the-problem`](video-prompts.md#01-the-problem) — plays before you read, ~50s hook.

---

Specifications drafted in isolation reliably have blind spots. Not occasionally — reliably.

This is true regardless of what kind of project the spec belongs to. Nothing in this course is specific to software, or to marketing, or to any other domain — the CDP is a domain-agnostic protocol, and the problem it answers shows up identically wherever someone writes something meant to describe or prescribe something coherently. The pattern shows up across very different kinds of writing: a philosophical essay, a long-form book, a product spec, even the CDP's own manifesto. Each looked complete to the person who wrote it. Each missed things systematically, in ways the author could not see from inside their own head. This isn't a claim about carelessness. A careful author still misses — because the failure mode isn't attention, it's perspective. You cannot see your own blind spot by trying harder to look at it; that's what makes it a blind spot.

What the author intended often only becomes visible under adversarial pressure. Write something in one sitting and it reads complete — to you. The moment someone attacks it from an angle you didn't anticipate, gaps, ambiguities, and unexamined assumptions surface fast. That pattern has held across three domains applied by the same author (an essay, a book, the CDP itself) — not yet independently verified across other authors. It's a real but bounded evidence base, not a universal law; treat it as a strong working hypothesis, not settled fact.

## Why informal review doesn't fix this

Peer review works — when it's someone's actual job. Journals, regulated industries, and paid editorial pipelines give reviewers the time an artifact deserves. Outside those settings, the typical author asks a friend or colleague for feedback and rarely gets review of the depth required. Favors aren't the right currency for sustained adversarial attention — your friend has fifteen minutes and doesn't want to be the person who tears your work apart.

The CDP doesn't replace institutional review where it already exists. It fills the gap where no institution does — and in that gap it catches what informal review reliably misses: not just errors of commission (things that are wrong), but errors of omission (things that were never written because they were never considered).

## Why this matters before you build

By the time a blind spot surfaces on its own, the cost is already paid: wasted development cycles, a team that built the wrong thing, expectations that were never actually aligned. The earlier a gap is forced into visibility, the cheaper it is to close.

> "Demolish your specification before reality does. What survives the demolition is worth building."

That's the whole method in one sentence. The rest of this course is the mechanics of how.

## Check your understanding

- Why doesn't "just be more careful" fix the blind-spot problem?
- What's the difference between an error of commission and an error of omission, and which one is harder to catch through ordinary proofreading?
